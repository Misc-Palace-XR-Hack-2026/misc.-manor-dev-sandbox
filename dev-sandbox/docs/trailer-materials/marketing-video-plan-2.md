# Cozy Palace — a place for the things you meant to come back to

## The idea

A saved link becomes something with a place in a room. German invites us into his Cozy Palace, shows us one thing he kept, and leaves us imagining what we would put in ours.

The audience is someone who saves a song, a useful page, or a link from a friend because it means something, then leaves it in a growing list. The concrete value is personal context: give that link a recognizable object and a chosen spot, then return to that spot to open it. The room makes arranging and revisiting a small collection part of the experience. We are not promising that it is faster than search or a replacement for every bookmark.

**Positioning:** Cozy Palace lets you give the links and memories you care about a place in a room you make your own.

Here, "memories" means the associations people bring to their saved material, not a claim about improved recall. Avoid promises about memory training, anxiety, sleep, health, permanent storage, or never losing anything. Do not imply that linking a page archives its contents.

**Audience takeaway:** "I could make a room for my favorite things from the internet."

**One next action:** Watch the demo. The trailer makes someone want a room; the separate demo shows how it works. This is a warm invitation with visible product evidence, not a presenter listing features or insisting the app is wonderful.

Branding in this trailer is **Cozy Palace** throughout. This document does not authorize changes to the app name, repository, domains, packages, or existing user data. The old plan is preserved unchanged in `marketing-video-plan.previous-direct-concept.md` for history only; its branding, availability claims, and VR exclusion are not current production instructions.

## German's performance and the room

- German is the narrator/presenter, not an instruction to imitate an accent. Keep his natural pronunciation and speaking rhythm. Warm, conversational, slightly amused by his own filing habits; no stage-announcer voice or forced whisper.
- Hold the pipe as a comfortable, unlit prop. No smoking is needed. Avoid using it to punctuate every line or turn the character into a caricature.
- Give the "Other" line a little air. The humor comes from recognition, not a punchline delivery. Keep the audience included rather than mocked for having messy bookmarks.
- The reference is permission to be patient and specific, not permission to imitate Joe Pera. Do not copy his voice, cadence, character, jokes, music, or signature phrasing. This script is original.
- Preferred setup: an actual cozy corner, warm practical lamp, chair, restrained background, camera near eye level. Keep German's face readable; cozy does not mean underexposed. Cut between this space and real app capture.

## What the separate demo must prove

The trailer answers "Why would I want this?" The demo answers "What can I actually do, and how?" Use the same song or recipe so the transition feels like continuing the visit.

The demo should show, in a clear working sequence:

1. Add a supported link, name it, and associate it with an available object using the actual UI.
2. Place it in the room and show the real save/return behavior. Explain any local-storage or persistence limits that apply; do not claim indefinite preservation.
3. Select it again and open the source. Show the browser handoff honestly, including the transition out of VR if relevant.
4. Show enough supported personalization to explain ownership of the room, without making the trailer carry every setting.
5. Identify the tested desktop/headset setup, controls, and current limits. Distinguish shipping behavior from experimental VR work.

If a required action fails in the build, fix or remove the claim before filming; do not hide it with an edit. The trailer can omit setup steps, but its central promise still needs a working demonstration.
